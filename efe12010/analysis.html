Finding 1 — the crowd and this desk disagree on the United States Grand Prix

OLBG's motor racing hub shows Max Verstappen as the most-tipped driver for Austin, with 13 of 38 tips. Our own line makes Kimi Antonelli the narrow favourite at 30.1% against Verstappen's 26.6%, because Antonelli leads the championship on 340 points to Verstappen's 221 while Verstappen leads on recent wins, two of the last five against Antonelli's two.

The two inputs pull in opposite directions and the split weighting lands them almost level. That is a genuine disagreement with the crowd rather than an error on either side, and it is the reason this desk's published motor racing call is a Verstappen podium at −246 rather than a race winner: the podium market is where an in-band price exists without having to resolve the disagreement.

Finding 2 — a timezone trap that silently corrupts every scraped start time

OLBG prints event clocks with no timezone marker anywhere in the page. The clock is rendered in the reader's own zone, which for this session resolved to Eastern Daylight Time. Three independent cross-checks settled it: a Towcester greyhound race shown as "Today 12:12" and already expired (17:12 BST = 16:12 UTC), World Series Game 1 shown as "Today 20:00" against the MLB Stats API's 2026-10-24T00:00:00Z, and Fils v Michelsen shown as "Today 12:30" against the ATP's "Not Before 18:30" Brussels time.

All three resolve to UTC−4. Two official sites were then found to geo-render the same way: wst.tv prints the Belfast evening session as "14:00" and cricket.com.au prints Australian fixtures in Eastern time too. That resolved an apparent conflict between the OLBG snooker clock and the World Snooker Tour draw — both are Eastern, both mean 18:00 UTC, and the match is in fact the 19:00 BST evening session.

Finding 3 — where the edges actually are this week

Nine of the thirteen high-confidence calls are totals, handicaps or derivative markets rather than straight winners, and that is not a stylistic choice. In the NHL the records are bunched: only Boston (8-2-1) and Tampa Bay (7-2-0) separate themselves far enough for a money line to clear the floor. Everywhere else the separation is in the scoring rates, not the win rates — Philadelphia have scored 1.67 goals a game, Los Angeles 2.50, and both numbers push their totals below the market's usual six and a half.

The same thing happens in the World Series. Milwaukee finished 103-59 against Tampa Bay's 98-64, but the Pythagorean lines (103-59 against 90-72) and the road records (48-33 against 43-38) put the sides much closer than the raw gap suggests, so the run line pays better than the money line.

Finding 4 — the price cap changes which selection gets published

Three fixtures produced a straight favourite shorter than −250 and were therefore held at Medium with the published selection moved to an in-band derivative: New Jersey against Los Angeles, Valencia at Paris, and Mark Williams against David Gilbert. In each case the desk prices the favourite as the clear winner, but the brief's band stops at −250, so publishing the money line would put a selection outside the stated window at the top of the page.

This is worth stating plainly because it changes the shape of the output: a desk that ignored the band would show more "winner" picks and fewer totals, and would look more confident than the evidence warrants.

What the evidence would not support

Cricket is the clearest example. Five fixtures were confirmed in full — match id, both teams, UTC start and venue — from Cricket Australia's own matches index, but that index publishes no ladder, no squad and no form figures, and no second official source was read inside this session. Every cricket market is therefore blank. The fixtures are published because they are confirmed; the prices are not, because they would have had to be invented.

The same logic applies to two tennis matches where the players sit outside the portion of the official rankings that was read, to Colorado's goals for and against, and to Jak Jones's second-round frame line. In each case the surrounding row is published and the missing cell is left empty and listed on the flagged page.
