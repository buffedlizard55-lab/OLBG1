Methodology

One rating per side, one normal distribution per match, fair prices with no margin. Everything on this page is a modelling choice, and every number in it is published so a price can be reproduced by hand.

1. How a rating becomes a price

  1. One rating per side (0–100). Derived only from the official input on that row — ladder position, record, ranking, seed or points differential. No tipster odds are read, and no rating is copied from a bookmaker.
  2. Edge = (home rating − away rating) + home-field adjustment — but only when both sides have a measured rating from the official source. If either side is unrated the strength term is set to zero rather than filled with a prior, so the price is the venue baseline and no tip is published. Filling a missing rating with a league average creates an edge that was never measured (F-26B-14).
  3. Expected margin μ = edge × slope. The slope converts a rating point into the sport’s own scoring unit, which is why a basketball price and a hockey price are not the same number.
  4. P(home wins) = Φ(μ / σ). σ is the sport’s scoring spread; a 17-point rating gap is a coin flip at a neutral venue before the home adjustment.
  5. Handicaps come from the same distribution: P(covers −L) = Φ((μ − L) / σ). The ladder is therefore internally consistent with the moneyline.
  6. Totals are priced around the sport’s own baseline total, with their own spread.
  7. Best-of-N sports (darts, snooker, tennis) use a binomial leg model instead of a margin, which produces correct-score and set/frame/leg handicaps.
  8. Price = 1 / probability, rounded to two decimals. No margin is added; the overround a bookmaker would charge is a separate business decision and is not baked in.

2. Per-sport assumptions

These are the values used for every row in that sport, and they are modelling choices, not statistics. For the normal model, σ is the scoring spread and the total market is quoted around its own baseline and spread. For the binomial sports (darts, snooker, tennis) a rating point is converted into a per-leg probability and the match price is built from the series length, so no total market is originated. 3. Confidence and the price window

The brief sets two rules that cannot both hold loosely: a selection with a 70% chance prices at −233, and a −250 line is a 71.4% chance. Read literally, a High pick therefore has to land between 70.0% and 71.4% — a 1.4-percentage-point band — because anything stronger is priced shorter than −250 and anything weaker fails the 70% test. That arithmetic, not a shortage of matches, is why 10 selections out of 120 reach High. The window is treated as the binding constraint: a stronger selection is still published, but on the handicap rung that lands inside the window rather than on a moneyline that cannot.

  • High — model probability ≥ 70% and price inside −250 to +10,000 (decimal 1.40 to 101.00), and the row was verified on 22 October, and both sides carry a measured official rating.
  • Medium — 55–70%, or a High selection that a cap (carried row, unrated side) holds down.
  • Low — below 55%.
  • No bet — nothing on the card clears a 52% coin flip inside the window. Published as no bet rather than forced.
  • One grading scale. The grade shown against a match-winner call uses exactly the same rules as a selection, so a 77% favourite priced at 1.30 is graded Medium: the view is strong, but it cannot be a High pick because the brief’s −250 floor excludes it. That is why the High count on the landing page equals the number of bettable selections, not the number of strong opinions.
  • Every priced match carries a labelled match-winner call with its own grade, so no match is left without a view of who wins.
  • Only the match winner and handicap ladders are eligible to become the desk selection. A total ladder can always manufacture a 70% quote by moving the line, which would be a fake edge; totals are published for reference.

4. What the model does not do

  • It does not price draws. Every non-binomial sport is priced two-way, so a draw is never quoted and a favourite’s probability is very slightly overstated. Draw-capable sports here are handball, Gaelic football, rugby union, rugby league and AFLW. Flagged as F-26B-06 and left for the next pricing pass rather than changed mid-snapshot.
  • It does not read market prices. These are our own lines; no bookmaker or exchange price is loaded, so nothing here is a value bet against a market.
  • It does not know about injuries, team news, weather or travel. The only inputs are the official record fields printed on each row.
  • “Recent match history” means what the governing body published in the captured input — a win-loss streak where the table carries one (the NHL rows show W3 and L4), a Round 1 result where the tournament page lists one (Wales Women 6-70, France 4-78), or a ladder record across the completed rounds (AFLW 5-5, EuroLeague 5-1). Where the source published only a ranking or a seed, the preview quotes that and does not invent a run of results.
  • It is a snapshot. 28 rows are carried from 21 October and capped at Medium; prices do not move after publication.
  • 39 of the 120 priced matches have at least one side with no captured rating. Their prices are the venue baseline only and they carry no tip; the table marks them “no rating captured” and each one is listed on the flagged page. That is deliberately visible rather than filled in: a fixture with a valid official link but no published rating is covered, not priced.
  • It is not a prediction service. A High grade means the model and the window agree, not that the selection will win.

5. Scope

In scope (17 sports): Tennis, Basketball, Golf, Cricket, Snooker, Rugby Union, Rugby League, Baseball, Ice Hockey, Motor Racing, Darts, Handball, Volleyball, Aussie Rules, eSports, Gaelic Football, Greyhounds.

Excluded by the brief (7): Football, Horse Racing, American Football, Boxing, Cycling, Specials, Hurling. These were enumerated during the reverse-engineering pass — they are real OLBG hubs (sport ids 1, 2, 11, 16, 17, 18, 26) — but nothing in them is priced or listed here.
