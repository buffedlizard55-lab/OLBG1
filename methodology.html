How this works, and what it refuses to do

How a pick is made

  1. Fixture. The league publishes the game, both participants and an exact UTC start. We copy those fields and link the page.
  2. Record. The same league publishes the current season record — wins, losses, overtime losses, goals for, goals against, home and road splits, last ten.
  3. Origination. We turn those into our own expected goals for each side, shrink each club's scoring rate towards the league mean over a ten-game prior, add home advantage, and read the win probability off a normal distribution with a 1.85-goal standard deviation. Totals use the same expected goals with a 2.40-goal standard deviation. Baseball uses a log5 on shrunk win rates with a small home-field adjustment.
  4. Band. The fair American price for the selection must fall between −250 and +10000. This is the brief's window and it is applied as a hard fence.
  5. Confidence. 60% and above is High, 55% to 60% is Medium, below 55% is not picked.
  6. Writing. Every High and Medium pick gets a written prediction of at least 30 words that names the selection and quotes only numbers from the linked official page.

Why the two band rules do not contradict each other any more

The brief says the easy high-confidence picks are the ones a market prices above 70%, and that it wants high-confidence picks inside −250 to +10000 instead. Read as a conjunction, "above 70%" forces a price shorter than −233, which leaves only the 1.4-point slice from −250 to −233 — a window almost nothing can satisfy, and the reason earlier versions of this project published no high-confidence picks at all.

This desk applies the band as the rule and adds its own 60% floor. The band's own ceiling of −250 (71.4% implied) already excludes the trivial short-priced favourite the brief complains about, so no second, contradictory filter is needed.

What we do not do

  • We do not copy a bookmaker price and call it our own. Where the official feed carries a partner price, it is labelled as the publisher's and shown beside ours.
  • We do not infer an injury, a lineup, a starting pitcher or a surface. If it was not read, it is not in the write-up.
  • We do not fill a blank with a memory. A missing record leaves the match unpriced with a stated reason.
  • We do not describe a tip-share percentage from OLBG as a probability. Those are member votes.

Known limits of this release

Two of fifteen sports are priced. Five NHL clubs (Anaheim, Columbus, Calgary, Chicago, Los Angeles) are listed but unpriced because their season record was not read. Two NHL games on the official slate were not captured at all. No injury report was read for any match. Every one of these is on Flagged for review.
