What is sound

  • Every register carries official links. The root desk and all namespaced desks link each row to a governing body, tour, federation or league endpoint. No aggregator is used to verify a fact.
  • Eleven validators, all passing. scripts/validate.py checks whole-tree link integrity, source-host allowlists and the primary desk's writing rules; scripts/final_audit.py and the per-session validators re-check their own namespaces. All of them pass on the tree this desk was built from.
  • No broken relative links. The gate resolves every anchor across every page in the repository; adding this desk's pages keeps that at zero.
  • Blank-and-flag discipline is real. Unconfirmed fields — starting goalies, probable pitchers, kick-off clocks for rolling sessions, ranks outside a published page — are empty on the rows and named in the flag lists, not filled in.
  • No stray build artefacts. No .pyc files are tracked, and .gitignore excludes them.

What is irregular

  • Fourteen parallel desks with overlapping fixtures. 10 fixtures on this desk alone are described differently by two registers; repository-wide, 180 pairings appear in two or more registers. Each desk solved the same problem independently, so the repository contained several defensible but mutually inconsistent readings of the same night's sport.
  • Stale fixtures are still priced on older desks. The root register (data/fixtures.json) still prices 44 fixtures dated before 22 October 2026. They are not wrong as records, but they are no longer bettable and a reader landing on those pages cannot tell.
  • The README's headline counts no longer match the tree. Its coverage summary describes 134 events and 160 published pages; the tree held 320 pages before this desk was added. The counts were accurate when written and have drifted with each new desk.
  • Two registers disagree about a fixture date. The World Series game five is dated 27 October by one register and 28 October by another; the desk publishes the later date with the disagreement logged.
  • Barça v Montpellier has two different scorelines. One register records the earlier meeting as 28-27, another as 34-27. Both cite the EHF. The desk quotes neither in its write-up.
  • Duplicate market families across desks. Handicap ladders, totals and outright boards are re-originated in several styles with different constants, so the same match can carry three different prices depending on which page a reader opens. This desk states its constants on Methodology for exactly that reason.
  • Coordination, not code, is the bottleneck. 2161 lines of task board record a dozen renumberings and collision resolutions. The structure held, but the same verification work was repeated many times over.

What this desk does about it

It reconciles every register it can reach into one dataset, logs every disagreement rather than resolving it silently, drops fixtures that have already started, states its model constants, and puts the band arithmetic where a reader can check it. It does not edit another session’s files: the fixes owed elsewhere are listed above with enough detail to action.
