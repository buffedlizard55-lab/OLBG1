How this desk works

The line-origination method

Prices are this desk's own estimates, converted to a margin-free two-way book (decimal = 1 / p). The formulas are fixed in advance and applied identically to every row:

  • Hockey: s = (points + 1) / (games + 2); logit(p_home) = 1.5 (s_home - s_away) + 0.12, with the home term dropped for the neutral-site Heritage Classic.
  • Baseball: s = ln(pct / (1 - pct)); logit(p_home) = 1.0 (s_home - s_away) + 0.20.
  • Rugby: s = (league points + 1) / (games + 2); logit(p_home) = 0.9 (s_home - s_away) + 0.10.

The shrinkage term stops a three-game record from producing an absurd price. The handicap line is derived from the same probability with a normal margin assumption (sigma 1.9 goals, 3.4 runs, 12 points) and is labelled a structural estimate wherever it appears.

The confidence rule, and its arithmetic

High requires both parts of the brief: an originated probability above 70% and a fair American price inside −250 to +10000. A fair price of −250 is 71.43%, so the qualifying window is 70.00% to 71.43%.

Strong is anything at 70% or better that lands outside that window — which in practice means the short-priced favourites the band excludes. Medium runs 55-70% and Low below 55%. Picks are not promoted to High to make the page look better: this desk publishes 1 High, 2 Strong, 3 Medium and 5 Low.

What a row must have before it is priced

  • The fixture on an official schedule with a start time that had not passed.
  • A numeric input for both sides from an official publisher.
  • A link to that publisher on the row itself.

If any of the three is missing the row stays blank and the missing input is named. That is why twelve of the fifteen sports have no price tonight.

Prose rules

Each write-up names the selection in its first clause, is at least 30 words, quotes only numbers that appear in the official source linked on the same card, and avoids internal vocabulary. The build fails if any write-up is shorter than 30 words or shares a six-word run with another.
