How the numbers are made

Our prices are our own estimates, built from official inputs and published with the arithmetic shown so you can disagree with them precisely.

The gate the brief asks for, and why it is nearly empty

The brief asks for picks with “a greater than 70% chance to win” that also “sit between −250 and +10000 odds”. Those two rules are almost mutually exclusive:

  • A 70.0% chance is a fair price of −100 × 0.70 / 0.30 = −233.
  • A −250 price is a chance of 250 / 350 = 71.4%.
  • So the only probabilities that satisfy both are those between 70.0% and 71.4% — a 1.4‑point window.

Today, with 7 priced matches and 31 originated market rows, 0 selections land inside that window. The highest probability we can defend is 65.1%.

We resolved it the only way that keeps the arithmetic honest: we publish our true probability, we label confidence on a stated scale (below), and we show you the strict-gate count rather than relabelling a price that breaks your own rule.

The confidence scale we used

  • High our probability 65% or better, and our fair price inside −250…+10000.
  • Medium 57.5% to 65%.
  • Low below 57.5%.
  • No line no official input was readable, so no probability was issued.

This is our own scale, not the market’s. It is deliberately not anchored on “the market says 70%”, which was the failure mode in earlier desks: a market-implied 70% is just a short price, not evidence.

NHL match-winner line

For each club we take games played, points percentage, goals for and goals against from the NHL’s own club statistics feed. Points percentage is shrunk toward .500 by eight games of prior — with only ten games played, an 8-1-1 start is partly luck — and goal differential per game is shrunk the same way. The two are combined as 1.6 × points index + 0.12 × goal differential, a home-ice term of 0.16 is added on the logit scale, and the result is passed through a logistic function. The away probability is simply one minus the home probability, because an NHL game cannot end level.

Calibration check, in public: on the six games where the league publishes partner prices, our line lands within 2.1 points of the de-vigged market on four of them, and differs by 5–6 points on the two where the clubs have the fewest games played. That is the expected behaviour of a form model, and it is the reason we are comfortable publishing its output — including the parts where it disagrees.

NHL handicap and total lines

We build a Poisson goal distribution for each side from goals for and against per game (geometric mean, capped between 1.6 and 4.4 goals), then scale the two rates symmetrically until the moneyline from the Poisson model equals the moneyline above. Only then do we read the puck line and the total off that distribution. This guarantees the derived markets cannot contradict the winner market, which is the usual failure when totals and moneylines are priced independently.

MLB match-winner line

Log5 on official splits: the home club’s home win percentage against the away club’s away win percentage. For World Series Game 2 that is Milwaukee’s 55-26 home record against Tampa Bay’s 43-38 road record, giving 65.1%. We do not adjust for the starting pitchers, because neither pitcher’s season statistics were read from an official source — naming them without adjusting for them is honest; adjusting from a guess would not be.

Best published price and edge

Where the league publishes partner prices (the NHL does), we take the longest available price across the two-way providers and name the book. We exclude Doxxbet, whose two prices sum to about 81% implied: that is a three-way 60-minute market, and mixing it in would manufacture a false under-round (flag F-321-10). Edge is our probability minus 1 / decimal price.

Reproducing all of it

The data and the arithmetic are both in the repository and run without network access: python3 scripts/s321b595c_data.py rewrites data/321b595c/, python3 scripts/s321b595c_build.py rebuilds this site, and python3 scripts/s321b595c_validate.py runs the checks listed below. The only non-reproducible part is the reading of live pages, which is why every read carries its UTC minute on the sources page.

Checks that run before publication

  • Every source id used by a row exists in the source register, and every register entry has a URL and a read time.
  • Every priced row carries at least one official source.
  • American and decimal prices are recomputed from the stated probability, both ways.
  • The confidence label is recomputed from the probability and must match the published label.
  • Every prediction is at least 30 words, contains its selection, and shares no eight-word run with any other.
  • The words “model” and “verified” never appear in a reader-facing prediction.
  • Every relative link and fragment on every page resolves, and every external link is absolute https.
  • The published JSON must match what the data script produces, so a page cannot drift from its data.
