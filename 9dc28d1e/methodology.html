Method

Three deliberately small models, each with its assumptions printed next to its output. The goal is a defensible price, not a replica of a betting market.

Ice hockey

Goals for and against per game are shrunk toward the league mean with a weight of 12.0 pseudo-games, then combined multiplicatively as attack × defence against the opponent, divided by the league mean. Home advantage multiplies the home expectation by 1.045 and the away expectation by 0.9550000000000001. Independent Poisson goal counts give the margin distribution. A regulation tie is split evenly between the two clubs for the moneyline and produces a one-goal margin for the puck line, which is why neither side can cover −1.5 from a tie.

EuroLeague

A club's rating is its points differential per game, shrunk toward zero by 4.0 pseudo-games. The rating gap is multiplied by 0.5 — a ten-game differential in October is not worth its full weight — and 2.5 points of home edge are added. Expected total points come from the two clubs' combined scoring and conceding rates. Margins are normal with a standard deviation of 11.0; totals are normal with 15.5.

Everything in this section is an assumption. The shrink weight and the rating multiplier were chosen so that the largest favourite on the slate prices near 82% rather than 97%, which is what the earlier multiplicative version produced. That choice is documented because it is a judgement, not a measurement.

Baseball

Pythagorean win rates with exponent 1.83 are combined with log5 and a home adjustment, giving the moneyline. Run expectations are the mean of the batting side's runs scored and the bowling side's runs allowed per game, with 0.15 runs of home edge; tied nine-inning games are split evenly and the distribution is renormalised. Totals and run lines use that same Poisson pair.

The tier rule

HIGHprobability > 70% and fair American inside −250…+10000 and a written prediction backed by player-level evidence from an official feed
MEDIUMprobability ≥ 65%, or one of the two High tests failed. The reason is stored on the row and shown in the data export.
LOWeverything else

The third High condition is stricter than the brief. It exists because the brief also requires each pick to name a player, and a pick whose player numbers were never read is not that.

Sensitivity

Every selection is re-priced across a grid: for hockey, shrinkage of 6/12/20 games × home multipliers of 1.00/1.045/1.08; for basketball, shrinkage of 2/4/8 × home edge 2.0/2.5/3.0 × margin σ of 10/11/12; for baseball, exponents of 1.70/1.83/2.00 × home edge of 0/0.15/0.30 runs. The published range is the min and max across the grid.
