The High-confidence gate, taken literally

The brief asks for picks with more than 70% probability and odds between −250 and +10000. Those two conditions are not independent, and the interaction is the most important thing on this desk.

1. The arithmetic

A fair American price and a probability are the same number written two ways. For any probability above 50%, the price is −100p/(1−p). Setting the price to the brief's short edge, −250, and solving gives p = 0.714286. So:

ConditionEquivalent probabilityEffect
Price no shorter than −250p ≤ 71.43%excludes every strong favourite
Probability greater than 70%price shorter than −233.33excludes everything near a coin flip
Both, together70.00% < p ≤ 71.43%a window 1.43 percentage points wide
Price of +10000p = 0.99%unreachable by any of these markets

The consequence is stark. Of 682 priced selections, 167 clear 70%. 5 of those also sit inside the price window, and 4 carry the player-level evidence the brief requires. The gate does not select the best picks; it selects the picks whose prices happen to fall in a narrow band.

3. Everything is fragile at this sample size

Each club has played between seven and eleven games. The models shrink those rates toward the league mean, but the choice of shrinkage constant still moves a probability by three to five points — wider than the 1.43-point window itself. The sensitivity column on every High row is the model re-run across that grid. Two of the three Highs leave the window at one end of it. That is not a defect of the picks; it is a property of asking for a 71% selection from a ten-game sample.

4. What would make this desk stronger

  • A settled-fixture backtest. None of the priced fixtures had finished when the desk was built, so no calibration statistic exists (F-9DC-09).
  • Official player availability. Not published in the feeds used here; a High built on a lineup that changes at warm-up is not a High.
  • A league-average run environment for MLB, which would let totals be priced instead of withheld.
