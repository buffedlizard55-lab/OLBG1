Summary / Method

How the numbers were made

1. Discovery, then verification

OLBG's fifteen hubs were read first, in the order the brief lists the sports. Each hub gives the events that carry a live market, the crowd's most-tipped selection and, for most sports, a tip share. That is discovery. Nothing from a hub is published as a fact.

The second pass fetches the governing body, league, tour or promoter page for the fixtures that matter, and only numbers visible on those pages are written into the desk. Where the second pass failed, the row keeps its market but loses its facts, and the failure goes on the review queue with the HTTP result.

2. Originating a price

No bookmaker price is copied. For the NHL the rating is a club's point percentage plus 0.035 times its goal difference per game, with a 0.15 logit home-ice adjustment; the money line is the logistic of three-point-two times the rating gap plus that adjustment. Where a club's record could not be read, a 0.550 point-percentage placeholder is used and the row says so. Match totals use each side's goals for and against per game averaged and a normal model with a 1.45-goal standard deviation. The puck line is the money-line probability less 0.22 in log-odds terms, floored at 30%.

For tennis the price is set from the two semi-final results read on the ATP scores page — who was seeded, who needed three sets, who retired. For baseball it is a deliberately conservative home-field estimate, because only one postseason game of form existed. For every sport where no official form page could be read, the price is the crowd's split shrunk 40% toward a coin flip, and the row is labelled sentiment, never official.

3. The confidence rule

LabelNeedsWhat it means in practice
high60% or better on our numbers, at least one side's form read from an official page this session, and a fair price inside −250 to +10000The pick is our strongest view and the price is one a bettor could actually take.
medium55% or better with an official input, or 60% or better where only the fixture is officialSame discipline, closer call or thinner evidence.
lowEverything else, including every row whose only input is the OLBG crowdPublished for completeness. Not a recommendation to stake on.
outside bandFair price shorter than −250 or longer than +10000The match is still listed, but the pick cannot be High. Boston and Edmonton are today's examples.

This is deliberately not the "greater than 70% chance the market gives it" shortcut the brief warns about. A −250 price is already 71.4%, so requiring more than 70% and a price inside the band leaves almost no room. Confidence here is about evidence plus price, and the probability is always printed next to the pick so a reader can apply a stricter rule if they prefer.

4. What is deliberately not claimed

  • No injuries. Not one injury or team-sheet claim is published, because no official team-news page was readable for these fixtures. The brief asks for verified injuries; the honest answer here is that none could be confirmed, so none appears.
  • No head-to-head history unless an official page printed it, which none did on these reads.
  • No conversion of OLBG's clock. The hub prints edge-local times with no zone label, so they are printed verbatim in a column that says so.
  • No padding. 31 of 56 picks are Low and are labelled as such rather than promoted.
