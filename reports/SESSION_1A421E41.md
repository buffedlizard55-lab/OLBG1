## 2. Reverse-engineering OLBG

Worked out by harvesting, not from documentation. Full detail on
`1a421e41/reverse-engineering.html`.

**URL architecture**

```
sport hub    https://www.olbg.com/betting-tips/<Sport_Slug>/<sport_id>
competition  https://www.olbg.com/betting-tips/<Sport_Slug>/<Comp_Slug>/<id>
tip card     https://www.olbg.com/betting-tips/<Sport_Slug>/<Comp_Slug>/<Event_Slug>/<id>?event_id=NNN
```

`/Snooker/1` is served `/Snooker/8` — a wrong numeric id redirects to the correct slug rather than
erroring. The betting-tips sitemap is dominated by Horse Racing and the excluded sports and is a
dead end for enumerating fixtures; the hubs must be harvested individually.

**Card field semantics** (observed behaviour, published on the reverse-engineering page): day label
is a relative bucket with no absolute date anywhere on the hub; the clock is unlabelled; three
different separators (`vs`, `v`, `@`) occur and `@` reverses home/away order; the market chip is
empty on all six Gaelic Football cards; win/total tips are counts, not prices — OLBG publishes no
odds on the hub at all; consensus share is printed *blank*, not zero, when the win-tip count is 0.

**The tip-gated blind spot — the structurally important finding.** An OLBG card exists only once at
least one tipster has posted. A zero-tip fixture is invisible: no empty card, no placeholder,
nothing in the page source to enumerate. **OLBG is not a fixture list; it is a list of fixtures
somebody has tipped.** Proved three times against official slates on this single harvest:

* **Ice hockey.** NHL's official schedule API reports `numberOfGames: 12` for 2026-10-22. OLBG's hub
  renders 7 cards, all SHL or Liiga, and none of the 12 NHL games. (`F-1A-NHL-01`)
* **Basketball.** EuroLeague's official Round 7 has 9 upcoming games; OLBG renders 8.
  Paris Basketball v Valencia Basket, the round's closing game, is absent. (`F-1A-BBALL-01`)
* **Two whole sports.** The Aussie Rules hub (22) and eSports hub (23) both render "No tips found."
  (`F-1A-EMPTY-01`)

Practical consequence for any consumer: absence of a card is **not** evidence that a fixture does
not exist.

**The clock is US Eastern Daylight Time.** OLBG publishes no timezone anywhere. Cross-referencing
its card clocks against official EuroLeague kick-offs identifies it exactly: **all 8 EuroLeague
fixtures sit precisely 6 hours behind the official CEST time**, with no residual. CEST is UTC+2 and
EDT is UTC−4. (`F-1A-CLOCK-04`) A separate 5-hour internal inconsistency appears on the greyhound
hub, where the card title says `11:14 Kinsley` and the card clock says `Today 06:14`.
(`F-1A-CLOCK-01`)

## 3. Method

**Evidence classes.** A = an official body published a rating, record or seed for **both** sides.
B = one side only, hard-capped at Medium. C = no official source reached; OLBG consensus only,
hard-capped at Medium and never High.

**Four gates for High.** Verified fixture · Class A · official current form · p ≥ 0.700 **and**
zero-vig price inside −250 / +10000. Among qualifying rungs the tightest to our own centre wins.

**The window contradiction, resolved.** At zero vig, p = 0.700 prices at −233 and p = 0.714286
prices at exactly −250, so the two tests intersect in a band only **0.0143 wide**. At a 5%
overround they cannot both bind at all: p = 0.700 books at −277, already outside. We therefore gate
on the **zero-vig price we originate ourselves** and print the 5% book equivalent beside every price
for comparison only. It never decides anything.

**Home-court advantage is derived from official data, not assumed.** The official EuroLeague
standings contain 33 home wins and 28 away wins across 61 games → home win rate 0.540984 →
`HCA = sigma × Φ⁻¹(0.540984) = 1.18 points`. The sigma it multiplies is our assumption and is
labelled as such everywhere.

**Origination.** Where a body publishes points for and against, each side's expected score is the
mean of its own official scoring rate and its opponent's official points-allowed rate; the margin
centre is the difference plus HCA and the total centre is the sum. Where a body publishes only a
rating (seed, rank, top-16 status) we convert it with `centre = sigma × Φ⁻¹(p)` so the ladder is
still expressed in legs, frames or games.

**Refusals, published as flags rather than gaps.** A match total is originated **only** where a
governing body published the scoring inputs — otherwise the cell is blank and the row carries
`TOTAL-NOT-ORIGINATED`. A handicap ladder is originated only on an official rating input —
otherwise `SPREAD-NOT-ORIGINATED` and moneyline only. Every rung sits on a strict half-point grid,
so no rung can push.

## 4. The most important structural finding

**`F-1A-GRAN-01` — the high-confidence band is granularity-limited, not conviction-limited.**

A handicap rung steps probability by `0.3434 / sigma` per half point. Whether any rung lands inside
a 0.0143-wide band is therefore governed by the sport's scoring variance, not by how strong the
favourite is.

| Sport | σ | p-step per half rung | Qualifying rung |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basketball | 11.5 | 0.01493 | almost always |
| Rugby Union | 12.5 | 0.01374 | almost always |
| Aussie Rules | 32.0 | 0.00537 | almost always |
| Handball | 6.0 | 0.02862 | about half the time |
| Tennis (games) | 5.2 | 0.03302 | about half the time |
| Darts (legs) | 2.30 | 0.07465 | rarely |
| Snooker (frames) | 2.0 | 0.08585 | rarely |
| Volleyball (sets) | 1.15 | 0.14930 | rarely |

Consequence, stated plainly: a genuine **0.789** favourite in darts is rejected because the nearest
rung prices at −258, eight American points outside the window, while a basketball side at **0.701**
clears comfortably. That is a property of the rule, not a statement about which picks are more
likely to win. It is why every High pick on this desk comes from basketball plus one snooker frame
handicap, and it is why the rejected-Highs ledger is published in full rather than dropped.

## 7. Verification discipline

* Every row carries a source link. Where no official source exists the rating is Class C, the cell
  is blank, and the row is flagged — 31 rows carry an official source, the rest are explicitly
  flagged unverified rather than quietly filled in.
* No value is inferred, interpolated, or carried over from a bookmaker.
* No prediction is written over evidence weaker than Class A.
* Official URLs are rebuilt from harvested strings and labelled `olbg_url_reconstructed: true`;
  a reconstructed URL is never presented as a harvested literal.
* `scripts/s1a421e41_validate.py` re-derives every gate from the raw inputs rather than trusting the
  builder: price arithmetic, half-point grid, window flags, tightest-rung choice, the four High
  gates, the rejected ledger, prediction length and uniqueness, the standings reconciliation, the
  clock deltas, and every relative link in the built site.

## 11. Three defects found only by reading the deployed pages

All three shipped in PR #94, were invisible to every gate in the repository, and were caught by
fetching the live URLs and reading them. Recorded because the lesson generalises: a green validator
proves the data is consistent, not that the page reads correctly.

1. **Literal `%%` in rendered prose** (PR #98). Three strings were assembled without a `%` operator,
   so an escaped `%%` reached the browser: the High-confidence lede on `index.html`, the Rejected
   Highs heading on `window.html`, and the Rejected Highs lede on three sport pages. Valid HTML,
   valid prose, no gate could see it. Fixed at the generator so a rebuild cannot reintroduce it.

2. **Ladders emitted saturated rungs.** Both ladders ran to `3.0 × sigma` and kept emitting after the
   probability clipped, producing dozens of numerically identical rows at the 0.94 ceiling and the
   0.06 floor — one fixture printed 32 near-identical rejected rows. Both ladders now stop before the
   first saturated rung. Markets fell **2938 → 1642** and no rung sits on a clipped probability. The
   totals guard had to measure the *farther* side of the centre, because the ladder base sits on the
   half-point grid while the centre generally does not, so the low rungs saturate first.

3. **Handicap line signs contradicted their own labels.** `home −7.5` was stored as `+7.5` and
   `away +9.5` as `−9.5`. The sign now follows the label — home gives points, away receives them.
   `offset` is documented as the cushion between the line and our expected margin measured in the
   bet's direction, which is the quantity the tightest-rung rule actually compares.

The rejected-Highs ledger was also trimmed from **410 rows to 15**: it now prints only the
informative entries per fixture (the match winner when a genuine favourite is priced too short, and
the nearest miss to the −250 floor). The full count is kept on every row as `rejected_high_count` and
the complete ladder remains on the markets page, so nothing is silently dropped. `window.html` went
from 247 KB to 16 KB and the site from 4.2 MB to 2.4 MB.

**None of the three changed a single High pick or price.** All ten selections and their American
prices are identical before and after, which is the check that matters: these were presentation and
data-hygiene defects, not model defects.

The validator gained three gates so they cannot regress: no rung may sit on a clipped probability, a
handicap label's sign must agree with its stored line, and the ledger must stay readable with no
repeated kind.
