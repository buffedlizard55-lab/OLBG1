Analysis and findings

What the rebuild found, what it could not verify, and where the previous board was wrong. This page is the audit trail, not a tip sheet.

1. The band rule is almost self-cancelling

At fair prices, a 70% shot is 1.43 and a 71.4% shot is 1.40. Requiring both a model probability of 70% or more and a price of -250 or longer leaves a 1.4-point window. Applied to 120 priced matches, only 10 selections clear it. The practical effect is that "High" now means "top of the qualifying band", not "the desk is unusually confident". Anyone reading the label as a skill claim is misreading it, and the previous board's nineteen sub -250 High grades were a policy breach rather than a strength.

127 rows across all seventeen in-scope sports: 120 head-to-heads with 3,175 originated prices and 7 event cards where no head-to-head exists (four golf tournaments, Formula 1, League of Legends Worlds and the GBGB meeting list). 99 rows were re-verified today; 28 are carried and capped at Medium.

5. Modelling notes

One rating per side, taken only from the official record printed in the row (points percentage, league win rate, tournament seed, world ranking or IRL ranking). A 0-100 rating point converts to a scoring margin through a per-sport slope, then a Normal distribution prices the winner, a handicap ladder and a total ladder. Darts, snooker and tennis use a binomial leg model instead. Any side without a captured official rating takes a default of 58 and the match is capped at Medium. Every price is fair: 1/probability, no margin.
