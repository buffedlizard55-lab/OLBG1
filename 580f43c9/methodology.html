Method

How every number was produced

The formulas were fixed before any match was priced. No probability was tuned to reach a confidence tier, and no tier was adjusted to reach a quota.

1. Probability

  • Basketball — each record is shrunk toward .500 by six pseudo-games ((w+3)/(w+l+6)), combined with log5, then the home side's odds are multiplied by 1.35.
  • Ice hockey — points percentage, shrunk by (pts+3)/(2·gp+6), log5, home odds ×1.20.
  • Baseball — full-season win rate (162 games needs no shrinkage), log5, home odds ×1.12.
  • Gaelic football — three-game group record shrunk by four pseudo-games, log5, no home edge (the final is at a neutral county ground).
  • Tennis — a seed-anchored prior of 0.64 for the top seed against an unseeded player, capped at low confidence because no ranking points or head-to-head were readable.

2. Price

Fair decimal is 1/p with no margin. American is derived from the rounded decimal. The fair price is our own number: it says what we think the selection is worth, not what any bookmaker will lay.

3. Derivative markets

Hockey goals are Poisson with λ = (own goals for/game + opponent goals against/game)/2. The puck line is P(margin ≥ 2); the total is the sum of the two λ, published as the next half-goal line above it. Basketball spreads, baseball run lines and Gaelic handicaps are withheld because the scoring-rate inputs they need were not readable — see Markets.

4. Confidence gate

  • High — p > 70% and fair American inside −250…+10000 and both sides rest on read inputs.
  • Medium — 60–70%, or >70% but priced shorter than −250 (the band rule excludes it).
  • Low — below 60%, or a sport with incomplete inputs (tennis today).

Because −250 is 71.43%, the High tier can only contain selections between 70.00% and 71.43%. That is a property of the brief's own two rules, not of the data, and it is why the tier is empty today.

5. What we refuse to do

  • Fill an unread field with a plausible value.
  • Quote a bookmaker price we did not see.
  • Write a prediction for a match whose official facts we could not read.
  • Treat an OLBG listing as fixture evidence.
