Methodology

1. Discovery

OLBG's sport hubs were mapped to the fifteen required categories and used to enumerate candidate events only. OLBG tip percentages are votes, not probabilities, and no OLBG number reaches a published price or fact.

2. Fixture confirmation

Each candidate was re-read from the league, tour, governing body or club page listed on its row: participants, competition, venue and start time. Missing values stay blank and are flagged. Times are published in UTC exactly as the source listed them; where a source renders venue-local time the conversion is noted in that fixture's flag.

3. Pricing

Match-level estimates start from published records, tables and rankings. Two-way markets are built as complementary pairs: p and 1−p, priced at 1/p with no vig, so both sides sum to 1.000. Spread and total ladders come from a normal approximation around the expected margin and total with the sport's own scale and spread, stepped on the natural line grid. Set-based sports convert a match probability into a per-set probability by inverting a best-of-three or best-of-five set structure, then price set handicaps and totals from that. Matchup sports (golf, motor racing) price head-to-head and a stroke or position ladder.

4. Confidence

High requires both conditions in the brief: a strike rate above 70% and a fair American price inside −250…+10000. A 70% chance is about −233, so the band's short end binds; a selection at, say, 74% would price at about −285 and is therefore published as Medium rather than relabelled. Medium covers 50–70%. Low is anything below 50%.

5. Selection cap

Up to five High picks per sport, taken in probability order with one price per market per fixture. Sports whose evidence supports fewer qualifying prices publish fewer, and the gap is stated on the sport page and the overview.

6. Predictions

One hand-written read per High pick, at least thirty words, always naming the event and stating the side. The content is drawn from the official page linked on the row: records, standings, recent results, published scoring and reported availability. No two reads share a six-word run, and internal jargon is kept off the public pages.

7. Limits

These are research prices, not market prices. Sports where no official card could be reached — greyhounds in this window — publish nothing rather than a guess, and are listed on the flagged page. Team news is only cited where an official page stated it; absence of a reported injury is not a claim that a squad is fully fit.

8. Where to check the working

Every input traces to a page on the sources list, and each fixture row repeats its own link for manual review. The gate that produced these numbers is scripts/s4c4968fc_validate.py, which fails the build when a price, a pick or a prediction breaks a rule set out on this page.
