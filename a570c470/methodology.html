Methodology

Everything on this site is either a sourced fact or a labelled model output. This page states which is which, in enough detail that the arithmetic can be reproduced.

1. Strength inputs

Each side carries one strength figure on a 0–100 scale, derived only from the official figure recorded in the fixture's inputs block:

  • Ranking sports (tennis, snooker, darts, esports): strength = 100 − 18·ln(rank), using the rank printed on the signed draw sheet or the official ranking list. A rank of 1 is 100; a rank of 32 is about 38.
  • Table sports (ice hockey, rugby union, Aussie rules, handball, EuroLeague): league points as a share of the maximum available, plus a goal or point-difference term per game.
  • Record sports (baseball, NBA, cricket, volleyball): win rate with two pseudo-games of shrinkage, plus a run-difference term where the feed publishes one.
  • Single-fixture sports (RLWC26, Gaelic football): the sourced round-one or semi-final margin, mapped to a pseudo win rate around .500.
Documented assumptions. Where a ranking is not published for one side the model assumes rank {'tennis': 100, 'snooker': 64, 'darts': 33, 'esports': 5} for that sport and the row is demoted to Medium at best. Home advantage is a documented constant per sport (tennis 0, snooker 0, darts 0, esports 0, basketball 3.5, ice-hockey 6, and so on), not a measured figure. These are assumptions and are labelled as such.

2. One distribution per match

P(win) = Phi(edge / SCALE) with the published per-sport scale, and the same edge sets the expected margin, so the handicap and total ladders are derived from the same distribution as the moneyline rather than from separate guesses. Best-of-N sports solve for the per-leg probability that reproduces the match probability under the binomial model, then price every frame, leg and set market off that score matrix.

3. Prices are fair, and the margin is published

Every price is 1 / probability, rounded to two decimals, with no margin added. The desk also prints the price a bookmaker charging 5% would quote, which is fair / 1.05. Nothing on this site is a market price.

4. The -250 to +10000 band

The brief asks for High-confidence picks that win more than 70% of the time and sit between −250 and +10000. Those two conditions pull in opposite directions: a 70% chance is a fair price of 1.43 (−233), and any margin pushes the quoted price shorter than −250. The desk resolves it by treating the window as binding on the price it publishes, and by originating the line: for a strong favourite it quotes the handicap rung whose probability lands near 71%, which is inside the window, rather than the shorter moneyline. Every High pick therefore satisfies both tests on its published price, and the moneyline call is printed beside it so nothing is hidden.

5. Confidence tiers and caps

  • High — model probability at least 70% and published price inside 1.40–101 (-250 to +10000).
  • Medium — at least 55%.
  • Low — everything else.

Caps applied automatically: the NBA (one-game sample) at Medium, PlusLiga (one round) at Medium, esports (draw tier is seeding, not strength) at Medium, cricket and Gaelic football at Low. A row whose strength input is a documented assumption is capped at Medium. The cap and its reason are printed on every affected row.

6. Blank beats wrong

If a figure cannot be read on an official page it is left empty and the row is listed on Flagged for Review. 25 of 141 fixtures are published with no price at all for exactly this reason. No value on this site was filled in from memory.

Independent model output, not betting advice. Prices are our own fair-probability quotes with no margin; the 5% margin a bookmaker would need is shown beside them.
