Findings that change how the data must be read

1. The clock is Eastern Time, not UK time

Greyhound cards are named with the UK race time (6:01 Newcastle) but displayed as 13:01 — a seven-hour offset. NHL games at 17:00Z display as 13:07. Both are consistent with the page rendering US Eastern time. Every OLBG clock in this desk was therefore converted from ET, and converted again only where an official page gave the local time.

2. Golf renders an empty board

The Golf hub returns “No tips found” while still documenting outright, first-round-leader and top-region markets. This is a platform-availability fact, not evidence that no golf is being played.

3. Rugby League has no match-level card

The Rugby League hub shows only three 2027 outrights. The World Cup fixtures on this site come from NRL.com, not from OLBG.

4. Snooker shows one match

Only Mark Williams v Jak Jones appears, even though the tournament is at semi-final stage.

5. The cricket card conflicts with the official fixture hub

OLBG lists two CSA T20 fixtures for 25 October. Cricket South Africa's hub for the same window lists Colts three-day matches and a Pro 50 women's game. Both cannot be the same competition, so the rows are flagged.

6. Listed times can be wrong by an hour across a clock change

France moves from CEST to CET on 25 October. Top 14's own page shows 21:05 local for La Rochelle v Bordeaux-Bègles, which is 20:05Z; the OLBG card shows 16:05 ET, which is also 20:05Z. The two agree only once the clock change is applied. A desk that converted naively would publish 19:05Z.
