Ballyhale Shamrocks vs Tullaroan

▶ Pick: Ballyhale Shamrocks to win (Match winner)

Ballyhale Shamrocks to win another Kilkenny county final at Nowlan Park. The defending champions edged Thomastown 1-18 to 1-16 in the semi-final, TJ Reid accounting for eight points, and finals at this ground are practically a home fixture for them. Tullaroan, in a first decider since 1994 after beating Bennettsbridge 1-20 to 2-14 with Shane Walsh scoring nine points, carry the romantic vote but the shorter price belongs to Shamrocks.

Dr Crokes vs Kenmare Shamrocks

▶ Pick: Under 33.5 points (Match total points)

Under 33.5 points in the Kerry final at Fitzgerald Stadium, where county finals so often tighten into chess matches. Kenmare's route here was a 0-15 to 0-9 semi-final win over North Kerry, a total of 24 points with Sean O'Shea scoring eight, and a first-ever senior final for the Shamrocks brings nerves as well as noise. Dr Crokes have the craft to control tempo, and control usually means a modest scoreboard.

Nemo Rangers vs St Finbarr's

▶ Pick: St Finbarr's to win (Match winner)

St Finbarr's to beat Nemo Rangers in the Cork final for a second straight year, in the third consecutive meeting of the pair at this stage. Nemo have now reached five finals in a row but lost the last three, including last year's decider to the Barr's, and scraped past Carrigaline 1-15 to 0-16 in the semi-final. The Barr's swept Clonakilty aside, and recent final-day history between these two favours them.

Shelmaliers vs Rathnure

▶ Pick: Rathnure to win (Match winner)

Rathnure to end their twenty-year wait for a Wexford senior hurling title. The county's most decorated club with twenty titles produced the semi-final performance of the championship, dethroning holders St Martin's 0-21 to 0-15 after extra-time, a reversal of last year's final. Remarkably Rathnure lost senior status as recently as 2023, and momentum built that way is a powerful thing to carry into Wexford Park.

Na Piarsaigh vs Kilmallock

▶ Pick: Na Piarsaigh to win (Match winner)

Na Piarsaigh to win the Limerick final, a low-confidence call between the county's two permanent fixtures. The remarkable statistic around this pairing is its familiarity: Na Piarsaigh have reached at least the last four for a decade, and Kilmallock's run of semi-final appearances stretches back fifteen years. With so little separating them historically, the city club's deeper recent success at this venue earns a tentative vote and nothing more.
