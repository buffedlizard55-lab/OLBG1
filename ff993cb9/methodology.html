Method — how a price and a rating are made

1. Discovery (OLBG)

OLBG's fifteen sport hubs tell us which matches and which headline markets (“Win Match”) exist. That is all they are used for. Tip counts and percentages on OLBG are counts of member tips, not probabilities, and they never enter a price. The route map is on the Inside OLBG page.

2. Facts (official sources only)

For each candidate match we read the governing body / league / tour page linked on its row: fixture, venue, start time, season records, points and set totals, qualifying times, semi-final scores. If the input a price needs (for example both teams' records) was not on an official page as read, the match is listed with no price and the missing input named.

3. Originating the price

From those official figures we estimate a win/cover probability p for each selection (rating gaps from points/goals/set differentials, home advantage, head-to-head meeting in the same competition where an official table records it). The published price is the fair, margin-free conversion: decimal = 1/p; American = −100·p/(1−p) for favourites, +100·(1−p)/p for outsiders. Estimates are deliberately conservative early in a season — one or two games of form can never produce a rating above Medium on this desk.

4. Confidence and the −250…+10000 window

The brief asks for high-confidence picks that are both “better than 70%” and priced between −250 and +10000 (not short-priced market chalk). Applied honestly to a fair price, 70% and −250 meet at 71.4%, so High is necessarily a narrow slice. Where a side is stronger than that (India Women, New Zealand in the RLWC), this desk prices a handicap or team-total line that lands back inside the window instead of dressing up a −900 favourite as analysis.

5. The written prediction

Each pick carries a unique prediction of at least 30 words (this desk: 45–95 words) naming the selection, the team or player, current form, recent results and the specific official figures behind the percentage. Injury or lineup claims appear only where an official list was read (on this desk: the RLWC team lists). The build is machine-checked for word count, for repeated six-word phrases across predictions, and for the band and rating rules above.

6. What would change a price

Prices are frozen at the snapshot time on every page. Team news, a confirmed finalist (snooker), Game 2's result (both baseball series) and Sunday's results feeding later rounds (RLWC, Basel) all move fair prices — flagged where relevant so you can re-check before acting.
