Reverse-engineering OLBG

OLBG's betting-tips hierarchy is exposed cleanly in its XML sitemap. This is the full map, extracted from sitemap-betting-tips.xml — not guessed from the navigation, which is incomplete.

1. URL grammar

Sport landing page : https://www.olbg.com/betting-tips/{Sport_Name}/{sport_id}
Competition page   : https://www.olbg.com/betting-tips/{Sport}/{Region}/{Competition}/{sport_id}
Event page         : https://www.olbg.com/betting-tips/{Sport}/…/{Home}_v_{Away}/{sport_id}?event_id={id}
Tipster league     : https://www.olbg.com/best-tipsters/{Sport}/{sport_id}

The trailing digit is always the sport ID, which makes the whole tree crawlable from a single page per sport. Event slugs use an underscore-joined Home_v_Away pattern with a numeric event_id.

3. What the sitemap does not tell you

  • Hurling has no ID. Gaelic games are split as “Gaelic Football” (25) with hurling folded into the wider Gaelic-sports hub. Anyone scraping by numeric ID will miss hurling entirely.
  • UFC/MMA is not a sport here. Those markets are filed under Boxing (16).
  • Specials have no betting-tips page at all. They exist only in the blog taxonomy — TV, politics and awards markets are not part of the tips tree.
  • Greyhounds (28) is inconsistent. It appears in the sitemap and the tipster competition, but is absent from the /sitemap navigation block.

6. Structural lessons for a scraper

  • Enumerate sports from the sitemap, not the nav — the nav omits Greyhounds and never lists the IDs.
  • Do not assume sport IDs are contiguous: 1–5, 7–17, 20–23, 25, 28. Six IDs in the range are unused or retired.
  • Tipster consensus lives on the event page as “9/11 Win Tips”, i.e. selections ÷ tips — parse the fraction, not the percentage, because the percentage is rounded and can hide small samples.
  • There is no public JSON API. Everything is server-rendered HTML, which is why the sitemap is the reliable entry point.
