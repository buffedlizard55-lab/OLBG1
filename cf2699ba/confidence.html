Confidence

The grading rule is applied mechanically to every published price. It is deliberately hard to satisfy, and today nothing satisfies the top tier. This page shows the rule, the distribution, and exactly which selections fell where and why.

The rule, as written

  • High — probability at least 70% and American price between −250 and +10,000 and the inputs came from an official page read this session.
  • Medium — probability between 62% and 70%, official inputs.
  • Low — everything else, including every indicative price built from tip shares.

Because the price floor and the probability floor meet, the eligible High band is only 70.0%–71.4%. A 75% shot is too short at roughly −300; a 90% shot is far too short. That is a property of the brief's own definition, not of this desk.

Why the confirmed set is so small

A fixture only enters this section if an official page named it, dated it and gave us an input. On 23 October that was two NHL games from the league's schedule feed, one MLB World Series game from MLB's schedule and statistics services, and three United Rugby Championship matches from the competition's own match centre. Everything else failed one of those three tests, and each failure is recorded by name on Flagged for review.

It is worth being blunt about the size of the sample. Six fixtures cannot produce five picks per sport, and no amount of writing can change that. What the desk can do — and did — is publish 107 clearly labelled indicative prices so the whole board is visible, keep every one of them at Low, and explain the arithmetic instead of hiding it.
