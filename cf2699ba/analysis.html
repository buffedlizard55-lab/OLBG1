Analysis

What the reverse-engineering pass found, what it could not find, and what would change the conclusions.

1. The OLBG surface is a discovery layer, and a leaky one

Every in-scope sport resolves through the same template: a sport hub keyed on a case-sensitive slug and an integer id, event cards with a top tip, a market name, a tip count and a percentage, and an event route that can be opened for the full market list. That structure held for all fifteen hubs. What it does not give you is anything you can rely on: the tip percentage is a vote share, the time has no timezone, and the list is not cleaned of games that have already started or, in the NHL's case, games that do not exist on the official slate (F-CF-04).

2. Time handling is the sharpest defect

Inside one capture, OLBG rendered a 17:12 Towcester race as 12:12 (a US-Eastern rendering), a set of URC matches at 14:45 that the URC itself prints as 14:45 local UK and Irish time, and a Formula 1 race at 16:00 that is 16:00 at the circuit in Austin. Any pipeline that adds a fixed offset to these strings will be wrong for at least two of the three. This desk publishes the string verbatim and takes UTC only from the governing body (F-CF-01).

3. The NHL feed contradicts itself on price

The league's schedule response embeds six prices per team. For Montreal at Chicago, three of them make Montreal a −162 to −170 favourite and three make Chicago a 1.59 favourite. There is no way to reconcile them from the payload, so the desk used only the three American-quote providers, published the contradiction on the row, and did not use any of it as a probability without saying so (F-CF-03).

5. The band problem the brief anticipated

The brief notes that many "high confidence" picks are simply short-priced favourites and asks for the −250 to +10,000 window. Applying that window honestly is what produced a zero. The desk's strongest single selection, Edinburgh at home to the Lions, lands around 85% and prices near −567: exactly the short favourite the window is designed to exclude. If the intent is "five picks a day that are likely to win", the window is not the binding constraint and should be relaxed; if the intent is value, then a 70% floor combined with a −250 floor is close to self-cancelling.

6. What would change these conclusions

  • An official team-news feed for each league, which is the single largest gap (F-CF-16).
  • Access to pdc.tv and wst.tv session pages, which would unlock darts and snooker pricing immediately.
  • A league table for the URC, the French and German handball leagues and PlusLiga, which would replace the one-round shrinkage with a real rating.
  • A second capture of the OLBG hubs later in the day, since several rows will expire and new ones appear.
